About

An open scholarly research platform

TehillimAI extends close reading, philology, and textual criticism through reproducible computational methods. It treats computational models as instruments for investigating the literary, linguistic, and textual complexity of the Psalms rather than as replacements for human interpretation.

A central concern is how digital representations preserve—or flatten—the ambiguity, wordplay, parallelism, syntax, discourse structure, and textual plurality that make Hebrew poetry so rich.

Research areas

Questions under investigation

Lexical similarity across the Psalter

An interactive tool comparing every psalm against every other by shared Hebrew vocabulary — a colorful similarity matrix, a network graph, and ranked nearest matches for any psalm.

Open the comparison tool →

Genre and literary structure

Computational clustering and similarity analysis examine how linguistic patterns relate to partial, overlapping, and contested scholarly classifications of the Psalms.

Hebrew poetry and ambiguity

The project studies parallelism, formulaic language, lexical ambiguity, wordplay, poetic syntax, and the capacity of computational models to preserve multiple plausible readings.

Manuscripts and textual traditions

Comparative analysis of the Masoretic Psalter, the Great Psalms Scroll, other Dead Sea Scroll manuscripts, the Septuagint, and the Peshitta investigates ordering, editorial logic, variation, and transmission.

AI-assisted Bible translation

Translation research evaluates how computational systems can support translators while preserving discourse, ambiguity, poetic form, and the interpretive possibilities of the source text.

Method

Representation before prediction

TehillimAI compares lexical, morphological, syntactic, discourse-based, graph-based, and neural representations of Biblical Hebrew. The aim is not merely to identify the highest-performing model, but to explain what each representation makes visible about the Psalter.

Research workflow

Open and reproducible scholarship

  1. 01

    Corpus and data model

    A modular data model represents Psalms, textual witnesses, linguistic annotations, ordering, and manuscript metadata.

  2. 02

    Exploratory analysis

    Clustering, similarity analysis, sequence comparison, and poetic feature analysis investigate literary and manuscript relationships.

  3. 03

    Philological evaluation

    Computational results are interpreted against Hebrew poetics, textual criticism, translation studies, and the history of Psalms scholarship.

  4. 04

    Open scholarship

    Code, datasets, notebooks, visualizations, preprints, and peer-reviewed research make the work transparent and reusable.
